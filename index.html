The AI Doc: Or How I Became an Apocaloptimist

Apocaloptimist: Someone who takes the severe risks of AI into consideration whilst still holding hope for a positive outcome.

The AI Doc: Or How I Became an Apocaloptimist is a documentary about a man who is soon to become a father and is worried about the life his child will have, if any, as AI becomes more and more powerful. He sets out to ease his worries by interviewing dozens of experts on AI.

Demis Hassabis

Leader

Demis Hassabis played chess as a child, and was disappointed in the way that everyone seemed to think that was the best use of their time. He learned to code, writing the script for parkgoers in the game Theme Park, and eventually gained a PhD in Cognitive Neuroscience. He then founded Google Deepmind and created AlphaFold, solving the 50-year protein folding problem. He now works at Isomorphic Labs, which he founded, to continue developing AI to solve similar problems.
  • Awarded Commander of the Order of the British Empire
  • Nobel Prize in Chemistry
"I've always believed in the power and potential of advanced AI to accelerate scientific discovery and greatly benefit society," Isomorphic Labs

Connor Leahy

Safety & Risk Researcher

When Connor was a student in the Technical University of Munich, he was fascinated with ChatGPT-2's ability to take raw data and make something out of it. He believed that solving intelligence would solve all other problems, so he was fascinated by AI. Connor had no understanding of it, and thought that taking GPT-2 apart and rebuilding it would help. Instead, Connor realized that despite how terrible GPT-2 was at making a coherent paragraph, he still had no idea how it made its decisions. So he began his path to regulating AI, joining ControlAI and founding Conjecture in an effort to educate everyone about the dangers of AI.
  • Reverse-engineered ChatGPT-2 overnight
  • Founded Conjecture
"AI is not a weapon, it is an adversary threatening humanity," Democracy Now!

David Evan Harris

Ethics, Fairness, & Critic

David Evan Harris is a professor from UC Berkeley. After working at Meta as a manager of multiple teams working on researching responsible AI development, social impact, and civic integrity, he knew more had to be done. David Evan began to travel and lecture multiple officials, lawmakers, and entire governments on tech policy and safe AI. He now teaches at Haas School of Business about ethical AI and social impact.
  • Advises governments on tech policy
  • Founded the Global Lives Project
"I'm not confident in even their ability to deal with the old types of threats," CNN

Tristan Harris

Commentator, Journalist, & Thinker

Born in San Francisco, Tristan Harris focuses on the impact of technology. After spending three years as a Google design ethicist and seeing his suggestions for less invasive technology get ignored, he took a different route. Tristan started a documentary called "The Social Dilemma", highlighting the impact of social media on youth and its dangers. He continues warning about the dangers of runaway tech, and co-founde the Center for Humane Technology to start working on safer technology.
  • Starred in "The Social Dilemma"
  • The closest thing Silicon Valley has to a conscience
"If you're not paying for the product, then you are the product," The AI Doc

Guillaume Verdon

Optimist & Accelerationist

Guillaume Verdon-Akzam, known online as "BasedBeffJezos", is a massive advocate for AI. After working for Alphabet X along with Google and NASA to make TensorFlow Quantum, he moved on to his own projects. Guillaume founded Extropic, a hardware company that focuses on making thermodynamic/probabilistic chips to maximise the efficiency and performance of AI. Guillaume believes rapid technological advancement and the full integration of AI into daily life will solve societal issues and human progress.
  • Co-created TensorFlow Quantum
  • Spearhead of e/acc, "effective acceleration"
“I don't think AI is going to kill us all. I think that sort of thinking is more dangerous than the AI itself,” The New York Post
